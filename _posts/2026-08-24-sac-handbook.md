After a year of calm and quiet, the season of vigorous campaigning, manifestos and *chapos* is back. ‘Tis the SAC election season.

As the election process gets underway, it is important to understand what this hustle-bustle is all about. Who are we voting for? What exactly do these posts entail? And what are the rules this time around?

Here’s your **Voter Handbook**, a quick guide to the posts, eligibility, voting and rules for SAC elections. For complete and official details, always refer to the **Election Commission’s guidelines**: [https://election.iitr.ac.in/5bf74ad4-6af9-40ca-85a1-d3545614393d/guidelines](https://election.iitr.ac.in/5bf74ad4-6af9-40ca-85a1-d3545614393d/guidelines)

### The Senate and the SAC
The Senate is the main decision-making body of IIT Roorkee for academic, instructional and research-related matters. Headed by the Director, the Senate meets at least four times a year and exercises its powers through various committees and bodies.  
The **Students’ Affairs Council (SAC)** functions under the overall supervision and control of the Senate. It acts as a collective forum for student concerns and deals with student welfare, discipline, co-curricular affairs, student housing and related matters. It also coordinates and reviews the functioning of various student bodies and plays a role in their budgets.

The **Students’ Senate**, a constituent body of the SAC, comprises:

* All elected and nominated Institute-level members  
* Elected members from each Bhawan and residential Assistant Wardens  
* Secretaries of other functional bodies of the SAC

## What happens after the elections?
The work does not end when the results are announced.

The SAC Executive Committee takes executive decisions on matters that do not require an immediate full SAC meeting, subject to subsequent consideration and approval by the SAC.

The elected representatives then spend their tenure working with the Students’ Senate, SAC, Bhawan representatives, faculty and Institute authorities on matters falling within their respective portfolios.

At the end of the tenure, elected members are required to document the initiatives undertaken during their term through the prescribed reporting process.

## Election Committee
The **Election Commission (EC)** supervises and conducts the elections, scrutinizes nominations, oversees the electoral process, handles election-related complaints and disputes, and enforces the Code of Conduct.

The EC retains primary authority over election-related matters. Grievances are to be raised through the designated complaint mechanism.

Each Bhawan also has a **Vigilance Committee**, headed by the Chief Warden, with the Assistant Warden and two student representatives, to help ensure free and fair elections.

## The Code of Conduct: Things You Should Know
**Disclaimer: This is a simplified summary for quick reference**. It **does not** replace the official rules. All candidates and students are strictly bound by the comprehensive [**Official Election Code of Conduct**](https://election.iitr.ac.in/5bf74ad4-6af9-40ca-85a1-d3545614393d/guidelines#code-of-conduct). Claiming ignorance of a rule omitted from this summary will not be accepted as a defense. In any dispute, the Official CoC supersedes this guide. 


### Campaigning

* Candidates must follow the prescribed campaign timings.  
* **iitr.ac.in** email IDs and Institute platforms cannot be used for campaigning.  
* Clubs, groups and club resources cannot be used for individual campaigns.  
* Public election-related gatherings of more than 5 people are prohibited.  
* Campaigning is online, except for EC-mandated offline debates.  
* Only EC-approved posters, templates and manifestos may be used.  
* Candidates must stick to their approved manifestos.


### No identity politics
Religion, region, language, caste, community, course, branch or stream cannot be used to solicit votes.


### No malicious anti-campaigning
Deliberately spreading false information, personal defamation or tampering with another candidate’s campaign material is prohibited.


### Silence & Polling Period Rules
During the campaign silence period **(and through the voting period where applicable)**: 

* No social-media campaigning, mass messaging, or organized canvassing.   
* No manifesto promotion  
* No vote poaching (this ban extends through the entire voting period).   
* Movement between Bhawans is strictly restricted from the end of the campaigning period until polling closes. Only EC members with official ID cards are exempt. 


### A few more things to remember
* No campaigning in LHC, the Library or Departments, or disruption of ongoing classes/tutorials/practicals.  
* No non-current student, including alumni, can participate in campaigning.  
* Office-bearers cannot support or criticize candidates.  
* Positions of power in clubs, Bhawans and mess committees cannot be used to influence elections.  
* Candidates are responsible for violations by supporters or anonymous handles; any such content must be disassociated from and publicly clarified within **3 hours**.   
* The EC does not recognize student groups or coalitions as official election parties.  
* No hard-copy election pamphlets may be distributed or pasted within IITR.


## First-Year Students: Special Rules
* No senior student, contesting or not, may bring a first-year student to their room.  
* First-years cannot enter senior messes until the elections are over.  
* Entry of non-residents into Rajendra and Sarojini Bhawan is restricted during the electoral process except for official election duty.  
  Any rule violation involving a senior and a first-year student will bypass normal channels and be handled directly by the **Convener** and **Head of Vigilance**. 


## What if someone violates the Code?
For contestants, penalties may include:

* Fines up to **₹5,000**  
* Cancellation of nomination  
* Immediate disqualification depending on severity  
* Disqualification after a second violation

For other students:

* **₹1,000** fine for the first social-media campaign violation during silence  
* **₹2,000** fine for further violation

Forcing someone to vote can attract a **₹2,000** fine and a Disciplinary Committee case.

Any attempt to access or tamper with the online voting system can lead to immediate disqualification and a Disciplinary Committee case.


## Offline Debates
Where mandated by the EC:

* If any candidate fails to appear for the scheduled offline debate, their candidature will be cancelled by the Election Commission (EC).
* Debates will be held only at approved venues and times.  
* An EC-appointed moderator will oversee proceedings.  
* Personal attacks and abusive language are prohibited.  
* Candidates must follow the prescribed format and receive equal speaking opportunities.  
* Unauthorized recording or distribution is prohibited.

## So, what are ya’ll voting for & why care?
At the end of the day, SAC elections are about choosing who you want speaking for you when student issues actually reach the Institute.

The manifestos will be full of ideas, promises and ambitious plans, but it is worth looking beyond the posters and asking a few simple questions:

_**Do they understand what their post actually involves?**_  
_**Can they realistically pull off what they’re promising?**_  
_**And, when the campaign is over, do you trust them to follow through?**_

The campaigning will last a few days. Their work, however, will last a whole tenure.

**So choose well… tada\!**
